# Pomodoro and focus sessions

The Pomodoro timer is at the top of the **Energy & focus** sidebar, above the [energy panel](../features/time-management.md#energy). Open it with **Open Pomodoro timer**, **Open energy panel** or the timer ribbon button. On mobile, **Mobile sidebar** in the Pomodoro settings chooses whether it opens in a tab or in the left or right sidebar. A workspace saved with the old Pomodoro tab opens the sidebar instead.

## Timer Interface

The timer has controls to start, pause, stop and skip breaks. While a timer is running, it shows both the remaining duration and the projected local finish time. You can also associate a task with the timer, and the time will be automatically recorded in the task's time tracking data when the session is complete.

## Focus sessions

A focus session times a whole task or timeblock instead of a single 25-minute timer. It takes the time planned for the item, splits it into chunks with breaks when it is longer than 30 minutes, and runs the chunks one after another. See [Focus sessions](../features/time-management.md#focus-sessions) for how the time is split.

Start one from:

- the **Up next** list in the sidebar, shown when no timer is running: a timeblock under way, then today's timeblocks and timed tasks still to come, then other tasks for today with an estimate;
- the ▶ button next to a task or timeblock in the energy panel's list of biggest effects;
- **Start focus session** in a task's context menu;
- the **Start focus session** command, which asks for a task.

During a session the sidebar shows the item's title, its energy kind and planned time, a bar with one segment per chunk, which chunk you are on, and when the session ends. **Skip chunk** (or **Skip break**) ends the current chunk and starts the next. **End session** stops the whole session.

## Menu bar

On desktop, **Show in the menu bar** in the Pomodoro settings also puts the timer, today's battery and the event, timeblock or timed task on now and next in the macOS menu bar (or the Windows and Linux notification area), with controls to pause, resume, skip a chunk and stop. See [Energy](../features/time-management.md#energy).

## Pomodoro Stats View

The Pomodoro Stats View provides analytics and historical data about your Pomodoro sessions. This includes a history of completed sessions, as well as metrics like completion rates and total time spent on tasks. Each chunk of a focus session is recorded as a Pomodoro session.
